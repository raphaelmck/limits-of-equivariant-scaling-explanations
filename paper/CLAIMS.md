# Claims and their supporting data

Each claim below is stated in the form the data supports, with the file that backs it. The last
section lists statements the same data does **not** support, which is the part worth reading
before extending any of this work.

Paths under `analysis_out/` are regenerated by `make analysis`; paths under `data/` are the
frozen inputs.

## Kernel claims

**K1. The four architectures scale differently in training compute.** Fitted exponents 0.227
(MPNN), 0.296 (MC-EGNN), 0.331 (GemNet-OC), 0.663 (eSEN), on the common compute interval
[8.49e15, 1.22e18] FLOPs.
`analysis_out/claim1/force_scaling_recomputed.csv`, from
`data/claim1/four_arch_force_checkpoint_table.csv`.

**K2. On the empirical frontier, GemNet-OC and eSEN reverse ordering near 1.24e17 FLOPs.** The
reversal is read off the observed checkpoints, not off the intersection of the fitted power laws;
the two crossing points differ, and only the observed one is treated as an event.
`analysis_out/claim1/frontier_points.csv`, `analysis_out/claim1/dense_grid_pairwise_gap_recomputed.csv`.

**K3. KRR built on the frozen force NTK recovers the broad architecture ordering.**
MPNN < MC-EGNN < {GemNet-OC, eSEN} in learning-curve area at all eight budgets.
`analysis_out/claim1/dense_grid_ranking_comparison_recomputed.csv`.

**K4. That same kernel does not track the reversal.** The neural gap changes sign once, between
1.15e17 and 1.24e17 FLOPs; the KRR gap is negative with a confidence interval excluding zero at
every one of the eight budgets, including the four above the crossover.
`analysis_out/claim1/dense_grid_pairwise_gap_recomputed.csv`.

## Intervention claims

**I1. Trained eSEN depends causally on its ell=4 features at block 9.** Damage increases with
perturbation magnitude at all four frontier checkpoints, with intervals excluding zero.
`analysis_out/claim2/frontier_ell4_degree_balanced_recomputed.json`.

**I2. That dependence is much weaker at the lowest-compute checkpoint than at the three higher
ones, and is not monotonic among the latter.**
Same file; the low-to-high contrast is what replicates, not an ordering of all four.

**I3. The sign of the low-to-high increase replicates across three independent training runs.**
Contrasts 0.477, 0.715, 0.134, each with an interval excluding zero.
`analysis_out/claim2/seed_replication_result_recomputed.json`.

**I4. The dependence is distributed across depth, not localized to one block.** Damage at matched
perturbation magnitude is non-monotonic in depth for three of the four checkpoints.
`analysis_out/claim2/depth_localization_recomputed.json`.

**I5. The low-to-high increase persists across held-out chemistry families, with heterogeneous
magnitude.** 0.427 for biomolecules, 0.344 for electrolytes, 0.149 for reactivity, against 0.565
on Neutral validation. Metal complexes (32 configurations) are excluded from inference.
`analysis_out/claim2/ood_shared_family_positive_control.csv`.

**I6. No matched-compute advantage for ell_max=4 is observed in the tested range.** 100 of 102
matched frontier pairs favour ell_max=2 on Neutral validation; all 102 favour it on
support-matched validation, every interval excluding zero. At the highest comparison covered by
both families the difference is not resolved: -0.0181, 95% CI [-0.0626, 0.0285].
`analysis_out/claim2/lmax24_all_frontier_summary.json`, `analysis_out/claim2/ood_lmax2_vs_lmax4_baseline.csv`.

The 102 pairs are **not** 102 independent experiments. They are densely sampled checkpoints from a
small number of training trajectories: the 126 ell_max=4 frontier checkpoints come from 11
trajectories, the 102 matched ones occupy nine, and the ell_max=2 side draws on 55 checkpoints
from eight. The counts are in the same summary file. Read the 102 as dense resolution along a few
trajectories, not as a sample size.

## Statements this data does not support

1. That the high-order pathway explains the scaling-exponent difference between any two
   architectures. This was never tested, and the direction of I6 is unfavourable to it.
2. That the high-order pathway explains an ell_max=4 advantage over matched ell_max=2. No such
   advantage is observed at any matched pair.
3. That dependence on high-order features grows *monotonically* with compute. The ordering
   reverses between the two highest checkpoints once perturbation magnitude is matched.
4. That ell_max=2 redistributes reliance onto lower degrees *because* it lacks higher ones. The
   same-width control does not let the effect be attributed to angular order.
5. That the *magnitude* of the low-to-high effect generalizes across training runs. Only its sign
   replicates; the magnitude spans 0.134 to 0.715 across three runs. A single run's value should
   never be quoted as representative without that range.
6. That tangent-kernel or spectral accounts are falsified. What fails is one specific surrogate --
   the frozen, full-output force NTK used as a *comparative* predictor across architectures.
7. That the 102 matched pairs constitute 102 independent observations. See the trajectory counts
   under I6; treating them as independent would overstate the precision of that comparison.
8. Anything phrased as proof of compensation. The evidence is consistent with redistribution, and
   that is as far as it goes.
